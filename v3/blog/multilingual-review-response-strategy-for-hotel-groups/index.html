Review management

A hotel group's guide to multilingual review replies

Keep your property's voice when your guests speak many languages. A practical approach to tone, context, and approval.

The Avarin teamSeptember 24, 20263 min read

In this article

  1. Start with the voice, before the language
  2. Keep each property's facts close
  3. Choose where your team reviews the draft
  4. Measure the follow-through

Start with the voice, before the language

A reply represents the property as much as a greeting at reception does. Before automating responses, agree on how the hotel sounds: warm or formal, concise or more conversational, signed by the manager or by the team.

Give the system useful examples and clear vocabulary. A small set of well-chosen replies can show how to acknowledge praise, respond to a concern, and close an answer without falling back on the same sentence every time.

Keep each property's facts close

A group may share a brand, but its hotels do not share every facility, policy, or service. A review about breakfast at one property should not receive information from another hotel's restaurant guide.

Use approved property knowledge and the review's actual content. Acknowledge what the guest said, and avoid promising a fix or claiming a staff action unless the underlying record supports it.

Choose where your team reviews the draft

Auto-publishing and approve-first workflows serve different needs. Decide which reviews your team wants to see before a reply becomes public, and make sure reviewers understand the guest's message as well as the proposed answer.

Translation can make the inbox easier to work with. The published reply should still read naturally in the guest's language, with the same attention to names, tone, and detail that your team would give a reply in its own language.

Measure the follow-through

Track the share of reviews answered and the time between a review and its response. Compare those measures across properties to find where a team needs support or where an approval queue is slowing things down.

Read a sample of replies regularly. Coverage tells you whether a reply was sent; the words tell you whether it was useful. Keep both views in the review process as your portfolio grows.
